How your week works

The part most career tools skip: what happens after you know the answer.

1Monday: three things

Your Flight Plan reads your roadmap and names the two or three tasks that actually move it this week. Not "build your network" — a named course, a specific deliverable, one person to email.

2All week: it chases

Put a date on a step and it becomes a commitment you get reminded about. Real application and fellowship deadlines land on your radar before they pass. Marco opens with what you said you would finish — not a blank box.

3Every month: the proof

What moved, what stalled, what you added to your evidence locker, which applications advanced. Career development you can actually show someone — a recruiter, an advisor, or yourself in March.

And you can check the answer

Every match opens up. No black box, no personality type, no four letters.

Your answers build a profile across 161 dimensions from O*NET — the occupational database published by the U.S. Department of Labor. Fit is the correlation between the shape of your profile and the shape of the occupation, mean-centered so that the things all work has in common cannot make everything look like a match.

That number is fit only: whether the work would suit you. What your coursework and projects actually prove is scored separately as readiness, and the two are never blended — a score you cannot take apart is a score you cannot argue with.

Questions people ask

Short answers, and links to the longer version where there is one.

What is FlightWay?

A career-development web app for students. It scores you against 780+ real occupations, shows the reasoning behind every match, and turns the career you pick into a roadmap with a few concrete tasks each week.

What do I actually get each week?

Two or three specific tasks pulled off your own roadmap — a named course, a deliverable to finish, a person to contact — plus any deadlines coming up and whatever you committed to last week. You check them off, and the following week is built from what you did.

Do I need to know what career I want first?

No. That is what the quiz is for. If you already have something in mind you can say so, and FlightWay starts from that instead of ignoring it.

Is the career quiz free?

Yes. Taking the quiz and seeing your top three matches costs nothing and does not require an account. A free account saves your results and shows all ten matches.

How long does the quiz take?

About ninety seconds. It is ten questions, and you can come back later to sharpen the result with more detail about your coursework and projects.

Where does FlightWay’s career data come from?

O*NET release 30.3, the occupational database published by the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration. It is public data describing what occupations involve and what they require. FlightWay is not endorsed by USDOL/ETA.

How is my fit score calculated?

Your answers build a profile across 161 O*NET dimensions, and fit is a mean-centered correlation between the shape of that profile and the shape of the occupation. Centering subtracts the profile all work has in common, which is what stops every career from looking like a match. Every match opens to show the dimensions that raised and lowered it.

Does the score mean I am qualified for the job?

No. The number shown is fit only, meaning whether the work suits you. Readiness, which is what your coursework, projects and resume actually prove, is scored separately and is never blended into fit.

Does FlightWay replace my school’s career advisor?

No, and it is not built to. Your career center holds information that is not in any dataset, including which employers recruit at your school and which alumni are reachable. FlightWay covers breadth and week-to-week follow-through.
